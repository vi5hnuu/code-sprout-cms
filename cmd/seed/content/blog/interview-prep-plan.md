Most interview preparation fails in one of two ways: grinding hundreds of random problems without learning the patterns behind them, or reading forever without solving anything. Here is a plan that balances both over eight weeks, at about an hour a day.

## Weeks 1–2: arrays, hashing and linear scans

Learn the patterns that appear in more than half of all interview questions.

- Read: [arrays](/dsa/arrays-introduction), [hash maps](/dsa/hash-maps-explained), [two pointers](/dsa/two-pointers-technique), [sliding window](/dsa/sliding-window-technique).
- Solve two to three problems a day, and say the complexity out loud before you code.

## Weeks 3–4: searching, sorting and stacks

- Read: [binary search](/dsa/binary-search), [sorting](/dsa/sorting-algorithms-compared), [binary search on the answer](/dsa/binary-search-on-the-answer).
- Practise writing binary search from memory until the boundaries feel boring.

## Weeks 5–6: dynamic programming and graphs

- Read: [introduction to DP](/dsa/introduction-to-dynamic-programming), [Kadane's algorithm](/dsa/kadanes-algorithm), [BFS](/dsa/breadth-first-search).
- For every DP problem, write the **state** and the **recurrence** in a comment before any code.

## Week 7: mixed practice

Solve problems **without knowing their category**. Recognising which pattern applies is the skill interviews actually test. Use the [Top Interview 10](/study-plans/top-interview-10) as a checkpoint.

## Week 8: mock interviews

- Solve problems against a 30-minute timer, talking through your reasoning.
- Revisit every problem you could not solve in weeks 1–7. Spaced repetition beats new volume.

:::callout{kind=tip}
When you are stuck for 20 minutes, read one hint, not the solution. Then come back to the problem two days later and solve it from scratch.
:::

## What to do on the day

1. Repeat the problem back, and ask about input sizes and edge cases.
2. State a brute-force solution and its complexity first.
3. Improve it, explaining each step.
4. Code it cleanly, then test it with a small example, including an edge case.

Good luck. You will be more ready than you feel.
